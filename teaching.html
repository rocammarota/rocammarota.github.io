COMPSCI 290P — Privacy-Enhancing Technologies
Foundations, Systems, and Applications

This graduate course examines how computing systems can derive value from sensitive data while limiting what is revealed during collection, communication, computation, storage, and release. Topics include differential privacy, secure multiparty computation, homomorphic encryption, zero-knowledge proofs, trusted execution environments, private information retrieval, oblivious computation, anonymous communication, and privacy-preserving machine learning.

The course treats privacy as a full-stack systems problem and combines technical lectures, research-paper discussion, invited speakers, project studios, and a quarter-long technical project.

Instructor: Ro Cammarota
Teaching Assistant: Caroline Wang
Format: In person, two 80-minute sessions per week
Units: 4

COMPSCI 231P — Parallel and Distributed Computing for Professionals

A graduate course in parallel and distributed computing tailored for professional computing programs, including the Master of Computer Science program. The course focuses on principles, abstractions, and systems techniques required to design and reason about modern parallel and distributed software.
