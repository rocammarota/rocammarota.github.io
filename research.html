Research

Research

Cryptography and computing systems designed for security, privacy, and trustworthy execution.

Post-Quantum Cryptography

I study the post-quantum transition as a systems problem. My work focuses on standards-aligned implementations, interoperability, cryptographic agility, performance, reproducible validation, and the software and architectural foundations needed for practical migration.

A central part of this work is understanding how post-quantum cryptography moves from standards and algorithms into real systems. This includes implementation correctness, protocol integration, performance and memory costs, software interfaces, hardware implications, and the ability of systems to evolve as standards and deployment requirements change.

Privacy-Enhancing Technologies

My research in privacy-enhancing technologies investigates how sensitive information can remain protected not only while it is stored or communicated, but also while it is being processed.

This work includes Fully Homomorphic Encryption, secure computation, trusted execution, privacy-preserving machine learning, and the software and hardware systems needed to make these technologies practical.

Confidential Intelligence

Confidential Intelligence asks a broader question: what forms of computation would we design if confidentiality were treated as a first-class design principle from the outset?

Rather than adapting algorithms designed for plaintext execution after the fact, I investigate the co-design of computational paradigms, data representations, cryptography, software systems, compilers, architectures, and hardware around the requirements of confidential execution.

Hyperdimensional Computing and Fully Homomorphic Encryption currently serve as one experimental vehicle for studying this question, particularly the relationship between computational structure and confidential execution.

Secure AI and Hardware Security

I also study how intelligent and cryptographic systems behave under physical and architectural attack.

This research includes side-channel analysis, fault injection, secure accelerators, architectural protection mechanisms, resilient computation, and the design of systems that preserve security assumptions even when the underlying hardware is observable or adversarially manipulated.

Systems and Architecture for Cryptography

Across these areas, I am interested in the systems consequences of cryptography: software architecture, programming models, compilers, runtime systems, memory organization, accelerators, and hardware-software co-design.

The common objective is to move security and privacy from peripheral mechanisms into fundamental properties of the computing stack.

Research Philosophy

My work emphasizes full-stack and experimentally reproducible research. Mathematical security guarantees are essential, but practical systems must also address implementation correctness, interoperability, performance, software interfaces, hardware behavior, side channels, and deployment.

My broader research mission is to make security and privacy guarantees foundational to modern computing .

Much of this work is carried out through the Confidential Intelligence Lab at UC Irvine.
